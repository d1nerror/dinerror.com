Krystallis Website

Role:
UI/UX designer
Scope:
UX strategy, information architecture,visual identity, wireframes (lo‑fi and hi‑fi), responsive design,interactive prototype

Problem

The tavern kept its tradition.The website didn’t.

Krystallis has served a traditional menu on Mt. Penteli for 77 years.Its website looked just as old, minus the charm.

The homepage was a photo gallery: slow to load, and only reachable by scrolling.Hours and contact details were split between the Contact page and a footer on every page,and that footer carried a gallery of six tiny archive thumbnails too.At that size nobody can see the photos, so they only add weight and push the real information out of view.

On mobile, the menu still wore the template’s default title, “Sidebar”,in English on a Greek site, in colors that failed contrast.The site was Greek only, so anyone who doesn’t read Greek couldn’t follow it or get in touch.And the menu? Nowhere.

Solution

Four pages.Everything else a scroll away.

The site now has four pages: Menu, Our story, Gallery and Contact.The gallery got a page of its own, where photos can be big and don’t slow anything down.Hours, phones, address and map sit in every footer, and Contact holds the full details.

Booking & language

One call away.In two languages.

Booking stays personal, by phone or email, and the path there is short:the numbers sit in every footer, and Contact adds the email and reservation details.Groups and events get their own details there.A Greek/English switch in the header lets visitors who don’t read Greek in, without losing their place.

Identity

Dressed like the tavern.

The colors come from the warmth of the tavern’s interior:wood, stone, and the glow of its fireplace.

Result

The menu is finally where a menu should be.

Mobile first, with everything in view right away.Four tidy pages, each with one job, in two languages.Tradition on the plate, clarity on the screen.
